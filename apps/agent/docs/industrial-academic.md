# Industrial Academic Palette

A compact palette for a restrained academic interface with industrial accents:
quiet paper surfaces, technical greens and blues, and warm yellow highlights for
selective emphasis.

## Colors

| Role | Name | Hex | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | Porcelain Paper | `#FFFDEF` | Page background, large calm surfaces |
| Primary Accent | Light Green | `#E3FBD7` | Soft highlights, subtle panels, positive emphasis |
| Primary Ink Accent | Dark Green | `#89A968` | Section labels, active states, quiet editorial marks |
| Ground | Dark Brown | `#615754` | Secondary text, rule work, low-contrast structure |
| Muted Ground | Light Brown | `#8D7A70` | Captions, metadata, inactive labels |
| Technical Accent | Light Blue | `#C5FCFB` | Diagrams, hover fills, data-like accents |
| Technical Ink | Dark Blue | `#5E8FA8` | Links, technical callouts, interaction emphasis |
| Warm Highlight | Light Yellow | `#FFF3B8` | Inline emphasis, annotations, citation-like highlights |
| Strong Highlight | Dark Yellow | `#FFC548` | Rare emphasis, badges, small high-signal moments |

## Suggested Mapping

Use `#FFFDEF` as the primary background and avoid heavy filled cards. Let borders
and spacing do most of the structure.

Use `#615754` and `#8D7A70` for the academic publication layer: captions,
metadata, divider lines, and quiet explanatory text.

Use `#89A968` as the main identity accent. It should appear more often than the
blue or yellow, but mostly in small typographic details.

Use `#5E8FA8` and `#C5FCFB` for technical moments: links, diagrams, code-adjacent
elements, or interactive states.

Use `#FFC548` sparingly. It should feel like an annotation, not a brand color.
